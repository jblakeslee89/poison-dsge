Try it yourself

The interactive companion below lets you move three sliders across the model’s parameter space and watch how the attack response changes. The defaults are set to the empirical (Smets-Wouters posterior) calibration.

The full interactive demo is hosted separately as a Plotly Dash app. While that app is being deployed, here are static versions of the key configurations:

The three canonical scenarios

Memo (toy model, 2025)

The original Spring 2025 calibration. Flat Phillips curve, no consumption habit, naive Fed. Produces the headline sign-flip on impact.

Realistic (Smets-Wouters posterior)

Empirical Phillips slope, Smets-Wouters consumption habit, naive Fed. Impact sign-flip attenuates; the cumulative two-year response is the real story.

Defended (heavy filter)

Realistic calibration with the Fed ignoring the raw nowcast signal entirely. The attack response collapses.

The defense-cost tradeoff

Welfare loss with vs without attack at the realistic-with-light-filter calibration (\(\kappa = 0.15\), \(h = 0.7\), \(K = 0.1\)). At \(K = 0\), the red bar collapses to the blue. But there is a cost: the Fed becomes less responsive to genuine real shocks.

The full Dash app (deployed at HuggingFace Spaces when the deployment finalizes) lets you sweep all three sliders and read off the welfare crossover probability live.
