# Dossier: Jennie W. Wenger

Senior Economist, RAND Corporation. Meeting prep for Wednesday.

- Defense personnel economist by trade. Twenty-year career arc built around DoD-facing labor-market and human-capital questions: recruiting, GI Bill returns, Coast Guard tempo, women in combat arms, cyber retention, military youth pipeline.

## Research throughlines

Five themes carry her work.

**1. National Guard Youth ChalleNGe evaluation (her career-defining series).** Twenty years of ROI and outcome evaluation of a DoD-sponsored residential program for high-school dropouts. Strongest single artifact: *Meeting the Challenge: The Economic Return on Investment in the National Guard Youth ChalleNGe Program* (RB-9641). Estimated $2.66 in lifetime benefits per $1 of program cost, a 166 percent ROI, raising lifetime earnings by roughly $43,514 (2010 dollars) per participant. Annual progress reports run RR-A2120 (2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25) and earlier RR-A882, RR-1848. She is now branching this into broader work on disconnected youth (PE-A4207-1, PE-A4207-2, both 2025), with Stephanie Bonds.

**2. Military education benefits and the Post-9/11 GI Bill.** Marquee paper: *Are Current Military Education Benefits Efficient and Effective for the Services?* (RR-1766-OSD, 2017), with Trey Miller, Matthew Baird, and others. One of RAND's most-cited assessments of GI Bill effects on recruiting and retention. Her 2022 JPAM paper, *"Veteran Educators or For-Profiteers? Tuition Responses to Changes in the Post-9/11 GI Bill"* (with Baird, Kofoed, Miller), is the most prestige-academic publication on her CV. Empirical study of how colleges raised tuition in response to Post-9/11 GI Bill rule changes. A Bennett-hypothesis paper applied to veterans.

**3. Personnel economics inside DoD.** Bread-and-butter work. *Retaining the Army's Cyber Expertise* (RR-1978-A, 2017). *Implications of Integrating Women into the Marine Corps Infantry* (2015). *Force Drawdowns and Demographic Diversity* (2015). *The Value of Experience in the Enlisted Force* (2018). *Balancing Quality of Life with Mission Requirements: An Analysis of Personnel Tempo on U.S. Coast Guard Major Cutters* (RR-2731-DHS, 2019). *Examination of Recent Deployment Experience Across the Services and Components* (RR-1928-A, 2018).

**4. Veterans' transitions and post-service labor markets.** *The Role of Education Benefits in Supporting Veterans as They Transition to Civilian Life* (2022). *The Role, Effectiveness, and Sustainability of Nonprofit Organizations That Provide Employment Support for Veterans* (2024). The MRDRC working paper *Long-Term Effects of Leaving Military Service in a Weak Economy* with Gutierrez documents persistent earnings and retirement-preparedness deficits for draft-era veterans entering weak labor markets.

**5. The new China and industrial-policy turn.** This is the most interesting recent development. *Factors Shaping the Future of China's Military* (RR-A2618-1, January 2025), co-authored with Mark Cozad. Applies her demographic and recruiting-economics lens to the PLA. Her own profile says "much of her recent research focuses on using economics to improve industrial policy and national security." This is the bridge between her old DoD-personnel work and the broader geoeconomics terrain.

**On AI and data integrity**: nothing in her published record on AI safety or data poisoning. The closest she gets is the 2016 *Searching for Information Online: Using Big Data to Identify the Concerns of Potential Army Recruits* (RR-1197-A, with Salar Jahedi and Douglas Yeung), which used Google Trends data on recruit-relevant queries. That is big-data-as-input, not data-integrity-as-vulnerability. Do not assume she has prior on AI policy.

## The Wenger-Egel question

There is no Wenger-Egel co-authored publication. I cross-checked Wenger's full RAND publication list (42-plus items, 2014-2026) against Egel's (45 items, 2014-2025). Zero overlap. External searches turned up nothing. Their research programs are essentially disjoint:

- Wenger lives in DoD personnel and human-capital economics.
- Egel lives in defense-applied development economics and irregular warfare. Israeli-Palestinian conflict cost calculators, Levant economic integration, USAID stabilization in Afghanistan, Yemen's Social Fund for Development, Special Operations effectiveness, central bank digital currencies and law enforcement, China economic competition. PhD UC Berkeley 2010 (Edward Miguel advisee). Professor at Pardee RAND. Director of RAND's Economic Strategy and Operations Unit.

Two plausible explanations for your memory of a collaboration. One: you are misremembering, perhaps from internal RAND committees or seminar appearances. Two: there is recent or unpublished joint work not yet indexed.

Recommended approach: do not walk in claiming you know they collaborate. Better is an open question: "Your work and Daniel Egel's both sit at the economics-and-national-security intersection. Do you work together much?" Lets her tell you the actual relationship.

## Methodological style

She is a reduced-form labor / applied-micro / program-evaluation economist. Toolkit:

- Cost-benefit and ROI analysis. ChalleNGe ROI is the cleanest example.
- Administrative data analysis. Deployment-experience and Coast Guard tempo work uses DoD personnel files.
- Survey design. *Understanding the Impact of DoD Youth Programs* (2024) surveyed school leaders. The 2025 male-mentors paper is survey-based.
- Quasi-experimental estimation in a labor-economics tradition. The JPAM tuition-response paper is the most academically rigorous example: a difference-in-differences-flavored identification.
- Big-data and search-query analytics. Once, in the 2016 Google Trends paper.

What she does not do: structural modeling, DSGE, calibration exercises, RCTs, formal theory. She is not a macroeconomist.

**Implication for showing her your DSGE work**: the methodological vocabulary will be foreign. She will not have a strong prior on Smets-Wouters frictions, habit formation, or NK Phillips curves. The parts of your project that will land with her are:

- Cost-benefit / policy-impact framing. What does data poisoning cost the Fed in misallocated capital?
- Empirical-identification angle. How would you actually detect this in the wild?
- Policy implications for industrial policy and defense.

Avoid leading with the Bayesian estimation. Lead with: "I have a defense-economics question I rebuilt as a structural sim because the reduced-form toy version was too brittle." That she will recognize.

## Conversation hooks (priority-ordered)

**1. The China demographics paper as bridge.** Her January 2025 *Factors Shaping the Future of China's Military* with Cozad is the one piece of her recent work that explicitly applies personnel economics to geoeconomic competition. Opener: "I read the China demographics piece. The way you used recruiting-pool analysis to think about PLA modernization is the kind of bridge between personnel economics and grand strategy I have not seen done well elsewhere. Is that a direction you see continuing?" Signals you read her recent work, opens a strategic-competition conversation, invites her to talk about the Cozad pairing (which is the actual cross-program collaboration she has, not the imagined Egel one).

**2. The disconnected-youth and recruiting-pool linkage.** Her 2025 disconnected-youth Perspectives are framed as a domestic well-being issue, but the same population is the binding constraint on military recruiting. The Army's qualified-recruit shortfall is downstream of the same demographic and labor-force phenomena. Ask: "Are you finding the disconnected-youth work is starting to talk to the recruiting-shortfall conversation? Those feel like the same population from two angles." Question only someone who actually understands DoD personnel economics would ask. Lets her show how she connects the two halves of her portfolio.

**3. The JPAM tuition-response paper as econ-cred handshake.** The 2022 *"Veteran Educators or For-Profiteers?"* paper is a clean Bennett-hypothesis identification. Most legible to an academic-track economist. If you want to signal "I came up through the same applied-micro tradition you did," ask about the identification strategy, or about whether she sees the Post-9/11 GI Bill tuition response as a case for benefit caps.

**4. The data-poisoning and Fed-nowcasting work, framed for her.** Do not lead with DSGE. Lead with: "I am rebuilding a Spring 2025 macro memo on AI data poisoning of Fed nowcasting into a structural sim. The toy version had a +1pp sign-flip that habit formation kills, but cumulative impact under SW frictions is about 5x larger, which I think is the real defense-economics story." Then ask: "On the personnel side, do you see analogous integrity questions in DoD's data infrastructure? Recruiting analytics, retention models, the pipeline you have used Google Trends data on?" Connects your work to hers via the data-integrity angle. Her 2016 Google Trends paper assumed clean signal. What happens if it is adversarially manipulated? Real research question, not a manufactured one. The kind of opening that turns a coffee into a follow-up.

**Optional fifth hook**: ask about the Wenger-Cozad collaboration specifically. Mark Cozad is a RAND political scientist on China. The fact that Wenger paired with him to apply personnel-economics tools to the PLA is the most interesting cross-program move on her recent CV. The closest analog to what an Egel collaboration would look like if it existed.
